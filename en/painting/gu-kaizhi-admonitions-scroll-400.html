Admonitions of the Instructress to the Court Ladies

中国 · 绢本设色

Admonitions of the Instructress to the Court Ladies is among the earliest surviving Chinese handscrolls of figure painting, generally regarded as a Tang-dynasty copy after Gu Kaizhi. It illustrates twelve passages from Zhang Hua's Admonitions in the form of nine surviving scenes: Lady Feng shielding the emperor from a bear, Lady Ban declining the imperial carriage, and court ladies at their toilet or seated in conversation. Draperies lift and flow; lines are fine and unbroken, like silk spun by a silkworm.

There is no perspective and no shadow, yet rhythm of line, distance between figures and blank space organise the narrative. It is both a moral textbook and an image-history of palace women's lives. It entered Britain after the Boxer upheaval in 1900 and is now in the British Museum.
